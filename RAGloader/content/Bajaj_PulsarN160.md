# Bajaj Pulsar N160 — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj positions the Pulsar N160 as the feature-flagship of its N-series naked line, aimed at riders in the 160cc bracket who want more technology than a typical commuter-naked offers.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj frames the N160's intended use as urban riding plus occasional highway use, describing it as a performance-oriented commuter. Its differentiator, per Bajaj, is spanning up to five trim levels across 2-valve and 4-valve engine variants, with mono- or dual-channel ABS depending on trim, and a top-trim TFT display offering Google Maps mirroring — features Bajaj presents as unusual at this displacement. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
Instrumentation varies significantly by trim: a monochrome LCD on the SD/TD trims, a 5-inch colour LCD on the S trim, and a 5-inch TFT display on the range-topping SS trim. Bajaj offers Bluetooth connectivity across all trims, with WiFi and Google Maps mirroring plus turn-by-turn navigation reserved for the SS trim — a tiered technology strategy Bajaj uses to differentiate trims within a single model line.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
This sentiment reflects aggregated opinion across the whole Pulsar (125-400cc range) family, not this specific model/variant, and comes from BikeWale/Team-BHP/MotorBeam/Cartoq-type sources, not Bajaj's own claims.

**Common praise (aggregated):** Engine refinement/exhaust note is often called best-in-class within Bajaj's own range (especially on the N250); strong low-end acceleration and city agility; long-term engine durability reported by very high-mileage owners (100,000+ km cases).

**Common complaints (aggregated):** Recurring electrical/CDI and speedometer faults on older-generation models; carburetion/fuel-injection driveability issues (rough idle, hesitation) tied to fuel quality; noticeable vibration above roughly 90 km/h on newer models (flagged in the N250 long-term test); clutch/gear-shift roughness in stop-go traffic; sporadic brake-related defects persisting through multiple service visits.

**Sources for this sentiment:** eAuto ('15 Common Pulsar Problems'), MotorBeam's Pulsar N250 long-term final report, a Team-BHP Pulsar 135 vibration thread, and a lowest-confidence Quora sentiment thread. (Tier 3/4).

## Competitors
Independent sources (BikeWale/ZigWheels, general segment framing) position the N160 against the lower trims of the Honda Unicorn/Hornet, the TVS Apache RTR 160, and the Yamaha FZ-S FI. These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
