# Bajaj Dominar 250 — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj markets the Dominar 250 with the tagline "Born To Sprint, Built To Tour," positioning it as the entry point into Bajaj's touring line for riders who want Dominar touring DNA at a lower displacement and price than the Dominar 400.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj frames the Dominar 250's intended use as highway touring plus confident city riding. Its differentiators, per Bajaj, are a twin-spar perimeter frame, 37mm USD front forks, a radial-caliper front disc, twin-channel switchable ABS, and switchable electronic traction control (ETC) available in Sport and Off-Road ride modes. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
The Dominar 250 uses a bonded digital speedometer with an integrated speedo flap, and offers 4 ride modes (Road, Rain, Sport, Off-Road) alongside switchable ETC. Lighting is full LED with an auto-headlamp-on function and 4 distinct headlamp modes. Bajaj markets the twin-spar perimeter frame, radial-caliper front brake, and twin-channel switchable ABS as bringing touring-flagship-level chassis and safety hardware down to the 250cc price point.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
This sentiment reflects aggregated opinion across the whole Dominar (250/400) family, not this specific model/variant, and comes from BikeWale/Team-BHP/MotorBeam/Cartoq-type sources, not Bajaj's own claims.

**Common praise (aggregated):** A 'big bike feel' and road presence disproportionate to price; confident braking (dual-channel ABS, large front disc); a torquey engine; reliability over high mileage reported by long-term owners; value-for-money framing versus the KTM 390 Duke.

**Common complaints (aggregated):** Persistent low/mid-RPM vibration is the single most repeated complaint (dealers reportedly offer an informal fix); an uncomfortable pillion seat; parking/low-speed handling challenged by the bike's weight; component niggles in long-term ownership (rear disc brake noise around 4,500-5,500 km, clutch cable replacement around 7,500 km, rocker-arm ticking); one forum thread flags rust/reliability regret on a single ABS unit.

**Sources for this sentiment:** Team-BHP's 10,000 km Dominar D400 review, a Cartoq piece on the vibration issue, and a Team-BHP 'Rust, Reliability & Regret' thread. (Tier 3/4).

## Competitors
Independent sources (a BikeWale 4-way comparison) treat the KTM 250 Duke as the Dominar 250's closest rival, with the Honda CB300R and KTM 200 Duke noted as adjacent options, and cross-shopping also reported against Bajaj's own Pulsar N250 and the Suzuki Gixxer 250. These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
