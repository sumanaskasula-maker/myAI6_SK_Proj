# KTM 390 Adventure X — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Positioning copy in the source brochures is near-identical to the 390 Adventure S ("ideal start... light, capable, confidence-building"), distinguished mainly by spec: cast alloy wheels and shorter suspension travel vs. the S's spoked wheels and longer travel. A separate 2025-update brochure emphasizes "Rally-Inspired Bodywork... inspired by KTM's Dakar racing heritage."

**Corporate note**: KTM is now a Bajaj Auto-owned brand in India (Bajaj Auto completed its acquisition of KTM per late-2025 reporting) — verify exact deal terms/date against Bajaj's own investor filings before treating as final. See KTM_BrandOverview.md for the full corporate-structure note.

## Use-Case Positioning
Positioned for riders who want 390 Adventure styling and a lower, more accessible seat height with cast-alloy, tubeless-tyre convenience — prioritizing low-maintenance road use over the R/S variants' off-road capability.

This reflects KTM's own marketing framing and this project's segment analysis, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
MTC, Quickshifter+, 3 Ride Modes, Cruise Control, Cornering ABS, Off-Road ABS, Ride-by-Wire, all-LED lighting w/ LED projector headlamps. 5" TFT with KTM Connect (turn-by-turn navigation, calls, music).

(Full structured specifications — engine, dimensions, brakes, suspension, warranty — are in `KTM_master_dataset.csv` / `KTM_master_dataset.xlsx`, not duplicated here.)

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
No independent, per-model owner-review sample (BikeWale/Team-BHP style) was gathered for this specific model in this project — see KTM_BrandOverview.md for the brand-wide, independently researched customer-sentiment findings (the India-specific "Chhapri"/"Khatam" social-stigma pattern and its likely origins, the gap between that social perception and independent professional/expert assessments of KTM's engineering, and general reliability/cost sentiment — shorter service intervals and pricier parts than Japanese rivals, and a dependence on diligent maintenance).
