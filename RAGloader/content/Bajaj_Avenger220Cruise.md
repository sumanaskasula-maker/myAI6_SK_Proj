# Bajaj Avenger 220 Cruise — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj positions the Avenger 220 Cruise as its touring-cruiser Avenger variant, aimed at riders who want a low, relaxed cruiser riding posture at a budget price point.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj frames the Avenger 220 Cruise's intended use as relaxed city and highway cruising. Its differentiators, per Bajaj, are a 30-degree low cruiser seating posture, forward-mounted foot pegs, and 5-step adjustable rear shock absorbers. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
Independent sources describe the Avenger 220 Cruise's instrumentation as a digital (LCD) unit covering speedometer, odometer, dual trip meters, fuel gauge, and clock, alongside auto-headlamp-on (AHO) and daytime running lights — no Bluetooth, navigation, or USB charging was found for this model by the sources reviewed. Bajaj's own marketing emphasis for this model centers on the relaxed cruiser riding position (30-degree seating posture, forward foot pegs) and the 5-step adjustable rear suspension, rather than connected-technology features.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
No customer-sentiment data exists for the Avenger family in the source material — this is stated plainly rather than fabricated.

## Competitors
Independent sources (ZigWheels, 91Wheels) most frequently cite the Royal Enfield Meteor 350 as the Avenger 220 Cruise's rival, with the Honda H'ness CB350 noted as a higher-price-step alternative — both compared under a "budget cruiser" framing relative to these 350cc rivals. These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
