# Bajaj Pulsar NS400Z — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj positions the Pulsar NS400Z as the range-topper of the entire Pulsar line, built around clutch-less "Sports Shift" gearing, aimed at enthusiasts moving up from 200-250cc machines who want a genuine performance naked.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj frames the NS400Z's intended use as spirited street riding with occasional track or highway use. Its differentiators, per Bajaj, are the Sports Shift clutch-less shifting system, a 320mm front disc with sintered brake pads, and 4 selectable ride modes including a switchable traction-control mode. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
The NS400Z's instrument console is a Bluetooth-enabled, bonded-glass LCD unit supporting call handling, music control, lap timing, and navigation — a feature set Bajaj positions as track-oriented as well as commuter-friendly. The headline mechanical feature is the Sports Shift system, which Bajaj markets as enabling clutch-less gear changes, paired with 4 ride modes and switchable traction control across all of them.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
This sentiment reflects aggregated opinion across the whole Pulsar (125-400cc range) family, not this specific model/variant, and comes from BikeWale/Team-BHP/MotorBeam/Cartoq-type sources, not Bajaj's own claims.

**Common praise (aggregated):** Engine refinement/exhaust note is often called best-in-class within Bajaj's own range (especially on the N250); strong low-end acceleration and city agility; long-term engine durability reported by very high-mileage owners (100,000+ km cases).

**Common complaints (aggregated):** Recurring electrical/CDI and speedometer faults on older-generation models; carburetion/fuel-injection driveability issues (rough idle, hesitation) tied to fuel quality; noticeable vibration above roughly 90 km/h on newer models (flagged in the N250 long-term test); clutch/gear-shift roughness in stop-go traffic; sporadic brake-related defects persisting through multiple service visits.

**Sources for this sentiment:** eAuto ('15 Common Pulsar Problems'), MotorBeam's Pulsar N250 long-term final report, a Team-BHP Pulsar 135 vibration thread, and a lowest-confidence Quora sentiment thread. (Tier 3/4).

## Competitors
Independent publications (BikeWale, Autocar India) treat the KTM 390 Duke as the NS400Z's primary benchmark rival, with Bajaj's own Dominar 400 noted as a sibling model serving a different use-case (touring rather than street performance). These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
