# TVS Ronin — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
TVS describes the Ronin as "A stylish 225cc motorcycle that's evolved like no other," positioning it as a neo-retro roadster for self-expression and lifestyle riders, sitting above TVS's commuter range and below the performance-oriented Apache range.

## Use-Case Positioning
TVS positions the Ronin as a neo-retro roadster for lifestyle and self-expression riders — primarily city cruising, with occasional weekend touring implied by the official touring-accessory range TVS sells for it (rear rack, backrest, touring kits). TVS does not market the Ronin outright as a highway tourer.

This reflects TVS's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
The Ronin runs a fully digital, connected speedometer with a digital fuel gauge, gear-shift indicator, and distance-to-empty readout. TVS's SmartXonnect suite on this model adds Bluetooth, voice assist, ride analysis and ride planning, social-media integration, last-parked-location tracking, and call/SMS alerts. Starting is via a "Silent Start" ISG (integrated starter-generator) feature rather than a conventional starter motor whine. Braking uses dual-channel ABS with distinct Rain and Urban calibrations. TVS also sells a range of official touring accessories for the Ronin through shop.tvsmotor.com — a rear rack, short and long touring kits, a backrest (offered in different pricing across the 2024 and 2025 editions), a radiator guard, a caliper guard, a lady-pillion footrest kit, and a tank grip — reinforcing its lifestyle/touring positioning.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
BikeWale aggregates a 4.6/5 rating from 1,152 ratings and 353 reviews — the highest aggregate rating among TVS models captured in this workbook. Commonly praised: comfort and ergonomics for both city and highway use, distinctive retro styling described by owners as "eye candy on the road," fuel efficiency of 40-50 km/l (up to roughly 52.5 km/l on the highway per some owners), strong perceived value against the Royal Enfield Hunter 350, and modern features (ABS, digital cluster, LEDs) that feel premium for the price. Commonly complained about: inconsistent dealer service experience and wait times, a small pillion seat, occasional gear/chain noise early in ownership, limited aftermarket accessory stock, and a low beam that owners feel could be brighter.

The following reflects aggregated owner and reviewer opinion from BikeWale/ZigWheels/Team-BHP, not TVS's own claims or independently verified fact.
