# Apache RR 310 — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
TVS's tagline for the RR 310 is "Crafted by champions. Crafted to conquer." TVS describes it as "the ultimate track weapon," born of what TVS calls TVS Racing's 35-year motorsport heritage, and positions it as the faired supersport sibling of the naked RTR 310.

## Use-Case Positioning
TVS positions the RR 310 explicitly as "the ultimate track weapon" — track and performance riding is TVS's own stated primary use case for this model, reinforced by a dedicated Track ride mode among its four ride modes (Urban, Rain, Sport, Track).

This reflects TVS's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
TVS states the RR 310 carries "first-in-segment" winglets that it says provide approximately 3 kg of aerodynamic downforce — a feature more commonly associated with larger supersport or MotoGP-derived machines. The model runs "Glide Through Technology Plus" (GTT+), an enhanced version of TVS's low-speed clutchless creep feature, alongside throttle-by-wire, a race-tuned slipper clutch, a dynamic rev-limit indicator, and an overspeed warning. Digi Docs support (storing RC/licence documents digitally) carries over from the wider Apache electronics suite. The instrument cluster is described by TVS as a SmartXonnect-enabled "Multi-Information Race Computer" with race analytics.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
No single aggregate star rating was captured for the RR 310 in this workbook; sentiment is drawn instead from a ZigWheels 9,000 km long-term review. Commonly praised: sharp design and strong fit/finish, a Bi-LED headlamp with a wide, strong beam spread, a touring-friendly riding position with generous seat space, well-balanced suspension between comfort and handling, no mechanical failures reported over 11 months of long-term use, and roughly 31 km/l fuel efficiency observed in that long-term test. Commonly complained about: noticeable vibration at 80-100 km/h cruising speeds, brakes described as lacking initial bite, ABS felt to intervene early, tyres called "lacklustre" for outright grip, awkward luggage mounting due to a short tail and angular tank, a fiddly single-button console for navigating the display, and the reviewer wanting more power and smoother low-rpm fuelling.

The following reflects aggregated owner and reviewer opinion from BikeWale/ZigWheels/Team-BHP, not TVS's own claims or independently verified fact.
