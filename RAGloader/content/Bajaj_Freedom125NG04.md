# Bajaj Freedom 125 NG04 — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj frames the Freedom 125 NG04 as "the world's first CNG motorcycle," positioned around roughly a 50% running-cost saving versus an equivalent petrol motorcycle. Bajaj's target customer, per its own model-identity materials, is the "cost-conscious, eco-aware daily commuter." The bike carries no separate marketing tagline beyond this CNG/economy framing on Bajaj's site.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj positions the Freedom 125 for daily city commuting by cost-conscious, high-mileage riders who want to minimize running cost. Bajaj calls out the mono-linked CNG cylinder plus a 2-litre petrol reserve tank as its key differentiator, and states there is no direct CNG rival in the market — Bajaj's own comparison set is limited to conventional 125cc petrol commuters (TVS Raider 125, Hero Xtreme 125R) and its own Pulsar NS125. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
The standout feature of the Freedom 125 is its bi-fuel CNG/petrol powertrain, marketed by Bajaj as a mono-linked CNG cylinder paired with a 2-litre auxiliary petrol reserve tank operated via a handlebar switch — a factory-standard feature rather than an aftermarket accessory. The instrument cluster is a fully digital speedometer, and the bike includes Bluetooth connectivity for call alerts with caller ID, missed-call indication, and battery-life display. Braking uses a Combi Braking System (CBS) rather than full ABS. Bajaj's press communications also cite CNG-specific environmental benefits versus petrol equivalents, though the exact percentage figures vary between press releases and are described by Bajaj itself as based on "internal evaluations" rather than an independently certified standard.

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
This sentiment reflects aggregated opinion across the whole Freedom 125 (CNG) family, not this specific model/variant, and comes from BikeWale/Team-BHP/MotorBeam/Cartoq-type sources, not Bajaj's own claims.

**Common praise (aggregated):** Genuinely competitive running-cost economics (cited as low as ₹1/km); mileage that broadly meets or exceeds the ARAI figure in city conditions per BikeWale's own test.

**Common complaints (aggregated):** Higher service costs than comparable 100cc petrol bikes (CNG-specific components plus periodic cylinder inspection); inconsistent/fluctuating RPM blamed on the injector/multi-sensor CNG system; repeated auto-choke engagement at cold start reducing real-world efficiency; at least one detailed review reports service-center technicians lacking training on CNG-specific issues. The sentiment sample is thin — this is a 2025-era launch, not a mature multi-year track record.

**Sources for this sentiment:** A BikeWale owner review ('Don't buy this bike' — a single detailed, anecdotal review) plus BikeWale and ZigWheels mileage test pieces. (Tier 3/4).

## Competitors
Because no direct CNG motorcycle rival exists in the Indian market, independent publications (Rushlane, ZigWheels) instead compare the Freedom 125 against conventional 125cc petrol commuters: the TVS Raider 125 and Hero Xtreme 125R, as well as Bajaj's own Pulsar NS125. These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
