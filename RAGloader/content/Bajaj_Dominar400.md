# Bajaj Dominar 400 — Knowledge Document

## Brand & Positioning
Bajaj positions the Dominar 400 as its flagship "big bike feel" tourer and, per Bajaj's own framing, its most powerful non-KTM-distributed model, aimed at touring enthusiasts and long-distance commuters who want torque plus comfort.

## Use-Case Positioning
Bajaj frames the Dominar 400's intended use as long-distance touring plus highway performance riding. Its differentiators, per Bajaj, are a twin-barrel exhaust, 43mm USD front forks, built-in bungee straps for luggage, switchable electronic traction control, and a dedicated factory touring-accessory range. This reflects Bajaj's own marketing framing, not independent test results.

## Notable Technology & Features
The Dominar 400 shares its bonded digital speedometer with integrated speedo flap and 4-ride-mode (Road/Rain/Sport/Off-Road) system with the Dominar 250, adding built-in bungee straps and a 4-mode LED headlamp. Bajaj also sells a factory-fitted "Touring Accessories" range for this model covering an engine bash plate, leg guard, hand guards, tall visor, navigation mount, USB charging port, a redesigned carrier with passenger backrest, and a separately-sold saddle stay for panniers (no prices published by Bajaj for these).

## Customer Sentiment (Aggregated Opinion — Not Verified Fact)
This sentiment reflects aggregated opinion across the whole Dominar (250/400) family, not this specific model/variant, and comes from BikeWale/Team-BHP/MotorBeam/Cartoq-type sources, not Bajaj's own claims.

**Common praise (aggregated):** A 'big bike feel' and road presence disproportionate to price; confident braking (dual-channel ABS, large front disc); a torquey engine; reliability over high mileage reported by long-term owners; value-for-money framing versus the KTM 390 Duke.

**Common complaints (aggregated):** Persistent low/mid-RPM vibration is the single most repeated complaint (dealers reportedly offer an informal fix); an uncomfortable pillion seat; parking/low-speed handling challenged by the bike's weight; component niggles in long-term ownership (rear disc brake noise around 4,500-5,500 km, clutch cable replacement around 7,500 km, rocker-arm ticking); one forum thread flags rust/reliability regret on a single ABS unit.

**Sources for this sentiment:** Team-BHP's 10,000 km Dominar D400 review, a Cartoq piece on the vibration issue, and a Team-BHP 'Rust, Reliability & Regret' thread. (Tier 3/4).

## Competitors
Independent sources (BikeWale, Autocar India, Outlook India) treat the KTM 390 Duke as the Dominar 400's segment-defining rival, and separately frame it as a touring alternative to the Royal Enfield Meteor 350, Classic 350, Himalayan, and Interceptor 650, as well as the Honda CB300F. These comparisons come from independent comparison articles, not from Bajaj itself.
